# Interview guide | Personas and pains discovery

**Date:** 05 Sep 26
**Method:** Generative discovery interview
**Length:** 60 minutes default, compressible to ~45. See **Time management** below — one document, not two guides.
**n=:** Target 4-6 new, independent participants this wave. Rolling review every 1-2 sessions — do not batch and review only at the end (see **Evidence → action trigger table**).

**Research questions:**
1. Do professionals whose core work involves consuming and analyzing news/information — in order to inform internal and/or external stakeholders' opinions, policies, and/or actions — share a similar pain shape across the consume → analyze → act loop, regardless of team size or tooling?
2. What personas exist within that population, and where do their pains converge or diverge?

**Hypothesis (researcher-only — visible to you, not leading to the participant):**
> Professionals who use news/information to inform stakeholders' opinions, policies, or actions share similar pains in how they consume, analyze, and act on it — regardless of team size or tools available to them.

**Falsifiability test (apply after each session, not during):** Code each participant's top-3 pains independently. **Confirmed** if ≥2 recurring themes appear across ≥3 distinct personas/roles. **Disconfirmed** if pains cluster entirely by persona/role with no cross-persona thematic overlap. A single clean disconfirming session (below) doesn't need to wait for full N to be actionable.

**Personas (path):** None — deliberately not anchored to prior persona/pain findings. See **Prep** below for how prior evidence is (and isn't) used.

**Decision this informs:** Could inform live modifications to Tempo **during** the pilot (which may run months, given this is a solo side project) — not just distant post-pilot roadmap. See **Evidence → action trigger table**.

## Recruiting & screening (lean — not a full screener doc)

**Channels:** professional network, warm intros, referrals from prior participants (see closing question 3). No cold/paid recruiting this wave.

**Pre-scheduling screener (4-5 questions, informal — email/DM is fine):**

1. "In your role, do you regularly use news or information to help shape decisions, opinions, or actions for others — a team, leadership, clients, or the public?" — **Include:** yes. **Exclude:** no.
2. "What's your role, and what organization or type of organization do you work for?" (generalizing is fine, e.g. "mid-size PR agency") — captured for participant classification.
3. "Roughly how big is the team you work within day to day?" — capture solo / small / large. Not a filter — used for moderator tagging.
4. "Roughly how many people outside your immediate team do you regularly work with on this?" — captured for participant classification and Branch prep. Not a filter.
5. "What tools, if any, do you currently use to track or analyze news/information?" — capture manual / some tools / advanced. Not a filter — used for moderator tagging.

## 0:00-0:05 — Intro and consent

Use your own voice:

- "Thanks for doing this. I want to understand how news and information move through your work — your environment, your habits, and what's hard about it."
- "This isn't a performance review or a pitch for a product; it's early-stage research to understand people's work, full stop."
- "I'll take notes. If we ever record or screenshot, I'll ask first. Notes stay internal to my research and get redacted before anything is shared."
- "Feel free to describe your organization generically if you'd rather not name it, and skip anything sensitive, confidential, or that names people you work with."
- "Any questions before we start?"

**Interviewer note:** Do not reference any product, prototype, or company name (ours or a competitor's) during this session. If asked what this is for, say it's early-stage research into how people handle information in their work, with specifics to come later if relevant.

---

## 0:05-0:12 — Warm-up

1. "So, tell me about your role — what do you do, and where do you work?" — *core*
2. "Walk me through a normal day or week in your role — what's the rhythm?" — *core*
3. "Tell me about the last time news or information significantly affected your work — what triggered it, what happened?" — *core*
4. "Who else is involved when that happens — your immediate team, anyone outside it?" — *cut order #2*
5. "Is that a typical, planned part of your day, or does it happen unpredictably?" — *cut order #1*

---

## 0:12-0:22 — Work habits

**Goal:** understand how they consume, analyze, and act on news/info day to day.
**Gap:** unknown whether this maps onto the same shape across different roles, team sizes, and tool sets.

1. "What role does news or information play in your work?" — *core*
2. "How often do you consume it, and how — sources, tools, channels?" — *core*
3. "How do you analyze or interpret it once you have it?" — *core, never cut. This stage maps most directly to Tempo's current interpretation-focused value prop (see [why this matters — field doctrine (archive)](../02-product/contracts/archive/why-this-matters--field-doctrine.md)) — understand it as deeply as any other question in this guide.*
4. "How do you act on it — what happens next, concretely?" — *core*

**Disconfirming probe** (core — always ask): "Was there a stretch of time — a week, a project — where news/information monitoring barely mattered to your work? What was different then?"

---

## 0:22-0:47 — Pain points

**Never cut this section, regardless of time pressure.**

**Goal:** identify top pains across the consume/analyze/act loop; test whether they converge across personas, team sizes, and tools.
**Gap:** unknown whether pains cluster by individual/role, or share a common shape across different professional contexts — this is the core hypothesis.

1. **Narrative open** — "What are the top three things that take the most time, create the most friction, or cause the most stress when you're consuming, analyzing, or acting on news/information?" — *core*
2. **Follow-up** — "Walk me through one recent example, start to finish." — *core*
3. **JTBD probe** — "When [that situation] happens, what are you ultimately trying to accomplish, and for whom?" (elicit a "when X, I want Y, so that Z" statement) — *core*
4. **Frequency/coping** — "How often does this happen?" + "What coping habits or workarounds do you use?" — *core*
5. **Convergence** — "If you had 100 points, how would you allocate them across your top three issues? Why?" — *core*
6. **Confirming → exceptions probe** — "You mentioned [top pain] is a big issue — has there been a recent time that wasn't a problem at all? What was different?" — *core*
7. **Disconfirming → root cause probe** — "Was there a recent week where none of these three were a real problem? What made that possible?" — *core*

---

## Branch: team size / tooling (only if time allows, or if clearly distinct from prior sessions)

- **If solo:** "Is there anyone you wish could share this workload? What would you hand off first?"
- **If team-based:** "How is this work divided across your team? Where do handoffs create friction?"
- **If low-tool/manual:** "Have you looked for tools to help with this? What stopped you from adopting one?"
- **If tool-using:** "What does your current tool do well? What do you still do manually around it?"

*Ask within the Work habits or Pain points time budget — don't add a separate timed block.*

---

## 0:47-0:53 — Closing

1. "Did we miss any issue that should be in your top three?" — *core*
2. "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one of these instantly, what would change for you day to day?" — *cut order #3*
3. "Do you know one or two other people who do similar work I could talk to?" — *core*

---

## 0:53-0:55 — Buffer / wrap

Use for anything that ran long, or to thank the participant and confirm any follow-up.

## Question rules

- No yes/no or leading questions.
- No product/company jargon — this guide never names Tempo or any prototype.
- One question at a time; let silence work.
- Mirror the participant's own words when following up.
- Stay in real, recent episodes; avoid hypotheticals until closing.
