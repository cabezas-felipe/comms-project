## How to read this brief

- **Fidelity tier** = how much credible public doctrine exists for *situation → readiness step* in that function:
  - `full` — multiple authoritative sources with explicit situation buckets and recommended practices.
  - `partial` — authoritative sources exist, but they cover process/format or an adjacent context (e.g. emergency response) more than the Tempo user's day-to-day situation.
  - `pointers` — only scattered or secondary sources.
  - `none` — nothing solid found.
- **Read depth.** "Read" means the page or PDF text was fetched and checked. "Search excerpt" means only the search-result excerpt was seen. Excerpts are enough to confirm a source exists and roughly what it covers. They are not enough to support detailed claims.
- **Observation vs inference.** Source descriptions are observations. Any statement about how a source *applies to Tempo* is marked **Inference**.
- **Evidence limits (applies to all four):** Nearly all sources are public-sector or IGO/NGO doctrine (US, UK, Canada, Ireland, NZ, UN). Tempo's research base is N=1 (a solo bilateral comms lead, Colombia–US; 🟠 Low confidence, per [Synthesis — personas and pains](../01-research/synthesis--personas-and-pains-discovery.md)). No Tempo participant has yet been interviewed in a policy, operations, or partnerships role, so whether these patterns fit those Tempo users is **untested**.
- **No quotes are invented.** The few short phrases in quotation marks were seen verbatim in fetched text. Everything else is paraphrase.

**Fidelity tier:** `full`

### Case families / situation buckets

1. **Narrative shift or new angle on an active story.** The story moved while the user is drafting or holding a line. Sources: PRIMER (monitoring, message drift), RESIST (situational insight).
2. **Inbound media request on a live issue.** Sources: PRSA first hour (holding statement), CERC working with media.
3. **Mis/disinformation affecting the user's organisation or issue.** Source: RESIST (recognise, then impact analysis, then response options including "no response").
4. **Escalation in severity or scope.** A local issue becomes cross-border or cross-agency. Source: GCS operating model activation levels.
5. **Correction needed on something already said.** Sources: PRSA code (prompt correction), PRIMER "Admit" guidance.
6. **Clearance-gated statement.** The response requires someone else's sign-off, for example embassy or HQ. Sources: FAM 10 / 3 FAM 4170, CERC release authority.

### Gaps / confidence

- Doctrine is crisis-heavy. Routine monitoring ("nothing big, but the framing moved") is covered only indirectly, mainly through PRIMER's monitoring and message-drift material. **Inference:** this routine case is the most common Tempo case, given the validated pain about monitoring narrative shifts while drafting.
- The bilateral, cross-language angle is not covered as such. FAM clearance shows that **local vs home-country audience** is a real distinction in diplomatic practice, but that is US-government internal policy, not a general rule for Tempo users.
- Confidence that the sources exist and say what is described: **high**. Confidence that the case families match Tempo comms users: 🟠 **Low**, N=1, consistent with the validated pain.

### Draft practice note body — communications

> **DRAFT — for PM truncate. Not SoT. English constraint pack for what comes next only.**
>
> **Posture:** You are helping a communications lead get *ready*, not telling them what to say or when. Frame any step as material they could prepare or a check they could run. The user, or whoever holds clearance, decides whether anything goes out.
>
> **Prefer:**
> - Readiness framing: what to have prepared (holding line, updated Q&A, a note to internal stakeholders) given what changed.
> - Being accurate and credible over being first. Flag unverified points as unverified.
> - Naming the audience or market a change most affects (e.g. home-country vs host-country media) when the inputs support it.
> - Pointing to whoever likely holds sign-off when a step depends on clearance, without assuming the outcome.
> - "Check whether the current line still holds" when the framing shifted but facts did not.
>
> **Avoid:**
> - Telling the user to send, publish, post, issue, respond, or decide. Never state when to release.
> - Writing the statement itself as if final.
> - Recommending a public rebuttal of mis/disinformation by default. "Prepare options, including not engaging" is the safer framing.
> - Retelling what happened or what changed.
> - Inventing stakeholders, deadlines, or clearance chains not present in the inputs.
>
> **Archetypes (evidence-supported):**
> 1. *Framing moved, facts didn't* → prepare a check of whether the existing line still fits. Optionally draft an alternate framing for review.
> 2. *Likely inbound press interest* → have a holding line and the known/unknown facts ready for whoever handles media.
> 3. *Possible correction* → gather what was said and what is now known, so a correction decision can be made quickly by the right person.
>

> - `cdc-cerc-manual` — https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/php/cerc-manual/
> - `cdc-cerc-crisis-plans-2014` — https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/media/pdfs/CERC_Crisis_Communication_Plans.pdf
> - `gcs-primer` — https://www.communications.gov.uk/publication/emergency-planning-framework-primer/

### Case families / situation buckets

1. **New or changed policy signal from an outside actor** (government, regulator, legislature, counterpart country). **Inference:** no source frames this situation directly. The closest are NZ DPMC's "Trigger" step and the Standards' Delivery pillar (monitoring and adapting).
2. **Decision-maker will need a brief or options.** Sources: CSPS, Ireland, DHS, NZ DPMC.
3. **Consultation or comment window opens.** Source: OECD Recommendation (consultation design, publishing analyses).
4. **Sensitive or unannounced policy.** Source: Open Policy Making toolkit (sensitive policy tools section; existence confirmed, contents not read).
5. **Time-compressed analysis** (24-hour case). Source: Open Policy Making toolkit.

### Gaps / confidence

- Sources are **strong on format and posture** (options, evidence, free and frank advice, the choice belongs to the decision-maker). They are **weak on situation → readiness step** for someone monitoring external policy moves. No public "if the counterpart government signals X, prepare Y" playbook turned up.
- Policy doctrine is written for civil servants advising ministers. **Inference:** Tempo policy users (e.g. advisers in an embassy, trade body, or NGO) likely play a similar "prepare options for the principal" role, but this is untested.
- The non-directive stance is **well supported**. CSPS and Ireland both say the advisor presents options and the leadership chooses, which maps cleanly onto pp5.
- Confidence that the sources exist and say what is described: **medium-high** (several are search excerpts only). Fit to Tempo users: 🟠 **Low**, with no policy participant yet.

### Draft practice note body — policy

> **DRAFT — for PM truncate. Not SoT. English constraint pack for what comes next only.**
>
> **Posture:** You support someone who advises decision-makers on policy. Help them be ready with evidence and options. The choice belongs to whoever they advise, never to Tempo or the user's draft.
>
> **Prefer:**
> - Naming what the change could mean for the user's priorities or stakeholders ("so what"), and what evidence would confirm it ("says who").
> - Suggesting material that could be prepared: a short options note (including status quo), a list of open questions, or the stakeholders whose positions may matter.
> - Flagging timing that the inputs support, such as a consultation window or scheduled vote. Never invent dates.
> - Balanced framing: note the downsides of the obvious response, not only the upsides.
>
> **Avoid:**
> - Recommending a position, decision, or course of action as the answer.
> - Telling the user to submit, file, publish, or brief anyone.
> - Asserting legal or regulatory effect beyond what the grounding states.
> - Retelling what happened or what changed.
> - Straw options (variations of one option dressed up as a choice).
>
> **Archetypes (partially evidence-supported):**
> 1. *External policy signal* → prepare a short "what this could mean / what we'd need to know" note for the principal.
> 2. *Decision likely needed upstream* → have credible options (including status quo) and their trade-offs ready.
>

> - `uk-open-policy-making-toolkit` — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/open-policy-making-toolkit

### Case families / situation buckets

1. **External event threatens continuity** of something the user runs (travel, events, logistics, service). Sources: NIMS/ICS situational awareness, Amber Book.
2. **Escalation threshold possibly crossed.** Sources: NCSC severity matrix, GCS activation levels (see Communications), ICS EEI "when to elevate".
3. **Multi-party coordination needed** (other teams or agencies involved). Sources: JESIP JDM, NIMS.
4. **Information is noisy or unverified.** Source: ICS EEI (separate critical from inaccurate or low-value information).
5. **Post-event review.** Sources: JDM "review", Amber Book "continuous improvement".

### Gaps / confidence

- The doctrine is **emergency-response and cyber-incident** doctrine. **Inference:** the patterns (situational awareness, escalation thresholds, options before action, decision log) transfer to everyday organisational operations, but the vocabulary (commanders, COBR, incident command) does not. Notes should carry the *pattern*, not the jargon.
- No public SOP was found for "non-emergency org operations lead reacting to news" (e.g. an event or programme manager).
- Doctrine assumes the operator *acts*. pp5 forbids Tempo from prescribing action, so the note must reduce these models to the situational-awareness and options-preparation stages only.
- Confidence that the sources exist and say what is described: **high**. Fit to Tempo ops users: 🟠 **Low**, with no ops participant yet.

### Draft practice note body — operations

> **DRAFT — for PM truncate. Not SoT. English constraint pack for what comes next only.**
>
> **Posture:** You support someone responsible for keeping work running (programmes, events, logistics, services). Help them see what might be affected and what they could have ready. Decisions to act, escalate, or change plans stay with them and their chain.
>
> **Prefer:**
> - Situational-awareness framing: what this change could put at risk for the user's work or stakeholders, and what isn't yet known.
> - Suggesting what could be checked or prepared: dependencies to confirm, contacts who may need to be in the loop, contingency options to have on hand.
> - Distinguishing confirmed facts from unverified reports.
> - Noting when a change *might* meet the user's own escalation threshold, without asserting that it does.
>
> **Avoid:**
> - Telling the user to cancel, reschedule, evacuate, escalate, or execute anything.
> - Emergency or command jargon (incident commander, COBR, activation) unless it appears in the user's own inputs.
> - Implying certainty about impact.
> - Retelling what happened or what changed.
>
> **Archetypes (evidence-supported as patterns; context transfer is inference):**
> 1. *Change may affect something the user runs* → note what could be at risk and which dependencies they might confirm.
> 2. *Possible escalation threshold* → have the facts and open questions ready in case it needs to go up the chain.
>

> - `fema-ics-ic-training-guide` — https://training.fema.gov/programs/nims-ics-position-specific/_docs/_pre-work/4950-ic-training-guide-pre-work.pdf

### Case families / situation buckets

From TPI Tool 5, unless noted otherwise.

1. **Change of counterpart or personnel at a partner.** TPI suggests respecting the history, rebuilding the case for the partnership, and asking questions.
2. **Partner not delivering.** First understand why (commitment, resources, competency, external or political factors), then solve it jointly.
3. **Global or HQ agreement not landing locally.** Rebuild around local interests and capacity.
4. **A partner's priorities or commitment level shifting.** Source: USAID MSI guidance (communicate changes early).
5. **Power imbalance or divergent interests surfacing.** Source: TPI Tool 6.
6. **External event that touches a partner's interests or reputation.** **Inference:** not directly covered. Closest are AA1000 Materiality and Responsiveness.

### Gaps / confidence

- Sources are written for **development-sector multi-stakeholder partnerships**. **Inference:** the relationship patterns (no blame, common understanding, partner-first framing, early notice of changes) generalise to corporate, embassy, or NGO partnership leads, but the SDG framing does not.
- The strongest situation → action material (TPI Tool 5) is about **internal partnership problems**. "News just broke that affects a partner" is the likely Tempo trigger, and it is the least covered.
- Confidence that the sources exist and say what is described: **high** for TPI (read), **medium** for others (search excerpts). Fit to Tempo users: 🟠 **Low**, with no partnerships participant yet.

### Draft practice note body — partnerships

> **DRAFT — for PM truncate. Not SoT. English constraint pack for what comes next only.**
>
> **Posture:** You support someone who manages relationships with partners and counterparts. Help them be ready for how a change could land with those partners. Treat partners as peers with their own interests. Decisions about outreach or commitments stay with the user.
>
> **Prefer:**
> - Naming which partners or stakeholders (from the user's inputs only) this change could matter to, and why from *their* side.
> - Suggesting what could be prepared: context a partner may ask about, open questions to understand their view, or a note on what is still unknown.
> - No-blame, joint-problem framing when the change involves a partner's difficulty or failure.
> - Noting when a counterpart change or a shift in priorities may mean the relationship needs re-grounding.
>
> **Avoid:**
> - Telling the user to contact, notify, reassure, renegotiate, or commit to anything.
> - Speculating about a partner's motives or internal situation beyond the grounding.
> - Adversarial or zero-sum framing.
> - Inventing partners or stakeholders not present in the inputs.
> - Retelling what happened or what changed.
>
> **Archetypes (partially evidence-supported):**
> 1. *Change touches a partner's interests* → prepare a view of how it may look from their side and what they might ask.
> 2. *New counterpart or shifting partner priorities* → have the history and the value of the partnership ready to revisit.
>

## Closing

### Tier summary

| Function | Tier | Main reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communications | `full` | Multiple authoritative crisis and comms doctrines plus diplomatic clearance rules, with clear situation buckets |
| Policy | `partial` | Strong format and posture doctrine (options; the choice is the principal's); thin on reacting to external policy signals |
| Operations | `partial` | Strong incident doctrine; context is emergency or cyber, so it transfers as a pattern only |
| Partnerships | `partial` | TPI troubleshooting gives real situation → response pairs, but for internal partnership problems in a development-sector frame |

### Recommended encode plan per function

These are recommendations; the PM decides.

- **Communications.** Encode a near-full note. Keep posture plus prefer/avoid plus 2–3 archetypes. This function has the best evidence and is closest to the validated N=1 pain (monitoring narrative shifts while drafting). Consider cutting the mis/disinformation archetype if the note is too long.
- **Policy.** Encode posture plus prefer/avoid. Keep at most one archetype ("external policy signal → what it could mean / what we'd need to know"). Its borrowed "options for the principal" posture is the most defensible part.
- **Operations.** Encode posture plus prefer/avoid only, written in plain organisational language with no incident-command vocabulary. Treat the archetypes as optional until an ops user is interviewed.
